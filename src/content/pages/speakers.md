Thank you for agreeing to speak at the Cosmology Seminar. This page collects the practical details so there are no surprises on the day. None of it is rigid; if you would like to do something different, just tell your host.

## Before your visit

Please send your host your name, affiliation, talk title, abstract, and a headshot for the announcement. Let them know about any accessibility needs or presentation requirements.

## The talk

**How long should it be?** Plan on about 45 minutes of talk with 15 minutes for questions. We keep the slot to an hour, and the discussion is usually the best part, so please do not squeeze a longer talk into the time.

**Who is in the room?** Graduate students, postdocs, and faculty from both the Physics and Astronomy departments, working across cosmology: the CMB, large-scale structure, gravitational lensing, dark matter and dark energy, galaxy formation, and the instruments and surveys behind them. Most people will not be specialists in your sub-field, so a few minutes of context at the start goes a long way, and please define any jargon that is specific to your area.

**How formal is it?** It is a seminar, not a colloquium. Expect questions during the talk, and feel free to pause and invite them. A talk that leaves room for discussion is more useful to the audience than a complete tour of every result.

**What should I bring?** Your own laptop is easiest. The room has a projector with HDMI and USB-C connections. A PDF copy of the slides on a USB stick or in your email is a good backup.

**Whom do I ask?** Your host for the week will be in touch before your visit. The organizers' emails are at the bottom of every page.

## Expenses

If travel reimbursement applies to your visit, your host confirms it with you when the invitation is finalized and will send the department's instructions for submitting receipts. Please keep itemized receipts for train tickets, taxis, and parking.
