title: Probing Baryon Feedback with the Kinematic Sunyaev-Zel’dovich Effect and Weak Lensing

Baryonic feedback can push gas out of the inner regions of haloes and into their outskirts and the surrounding cosmic web. This reshapes both the observable gas distribution and the small-scale matter distribution measured by weak lensing surveys. Hydrodynamical simulations that reproduce many galaxy properties still disagree on the strength of feedback, which makes baryonic effects a leading systematic for lensing cosmology. The kinematic Sunyaev-Zel’dovich (kSZ) effect is a direct probe of the density of this diffuse ionized gas.

In this talk, I will present a new model-independent measurement of the radial gas fraction around DESI galaxies, obtained by combining kSZ measurements with ACT with galaxy-galaxy weak lensing with HSC. By directly combining the kSZ and lensing measurements, we measure substantial gas depletion in the inner regions of haloes and its recovery toward larger scales without assuming a gas profile or halo-mass calibration. I will then compare these measurements with hydrodynamical simulations spanning a range of feedback prescriptions, and discuss how feedback, and other factors like stars and neutral gas shape the observed SZ signal and its interpretation. Together, these results provide a direct test of baryonic feedback models and help connect observations of circumgalactic gas to its impact on cosmology.
