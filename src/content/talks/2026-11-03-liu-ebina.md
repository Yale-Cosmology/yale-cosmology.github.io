Next-generation spectroscopic surveys will push beyond the reach of current experiments, targeting galaxies at z > 2. This high-redshift regime is attractive for several reasons, among them the opportunity to cross-correlate galaxy samples with CMB lensing near the peak of the lensing kernel. Lyman alpha emitters (LAEs) are one of the key tracers that will enable next-generation experiments to probe these redshifts, and with imaging preparation for such surveys already under way, we can begin targeting and observing LAEs now.

In this talk, I will demonstrate LAE target selection using medium-band imaging from the Intermediate Band Imaging Survey (IBIS). Using spectroscopic redshifts from Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument (DESI) follow-up observations, I will then present measurements of their 3D clustering, jointly fitting the LAE and QSO auto- and cross-spectra to constrain the LAE bias, Fingers-of-God effect, and the impact of radiative transfer. Our results demonstrate that LAEs can be reliably selected and modeled for next-generation surveys, marking necessary progress in the modeling preparation these experiments require.
